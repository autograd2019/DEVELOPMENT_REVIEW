## Executive Summary

Pembroke Construction and associated entities are seeking investment to deliver a **48-lot residential land subdivision** at Caboolture South, Queensland. The project consolidates three adjoining freehold parcels (181 Torrens Road, 85 River Drive and 3-7 Dorset Drive) within Moreton Bay City Council's **Next Generation Neighbourhood Precinct** — a designated infill growth area approximately 50 kilometres north of Brisbane CBD. All three sites are under contract, with settlements scheduled between May 2026 and March 2027.

A constructive **Prelodgement Meeting** was held with Council on 28 January 2026 (Council reference **DA/2026/0091**). Following that meeting and subsequent traffic engineering, the proposal was redesigned to consolidate the third parcel (3-7 Dorset Drive) and to take access from Dorset Drive. The **Development Application** has now been finalised for lodgement as a **Code Assessable Reconfiguring a Lot (3 into 48 lots on a Community Titles Scheme), including a private road and access easement**, supported by a full suite of technical reports (planning, civil, stormwater, flood, traffic and landscape).

## Introduction

This business case presents the investment opportunity for a 48-lot residential land subdivision spanning three adjoining parcels at 181 Torrens Road, 85 River Drive and 3-7 Dorset Drive, Caboolture South, Queensland. It sets out the project rationale, projected returns, development timeline, financing structure and the risk framework applied to test the project's resilience.

Cost estimates incorporate all material expenditure categories — land acquisition and stamp duty, holding and financing costs, consultant and statutory approval fees, civil and electrical construction, plan sealing, and sales and marketing — based on the consolidated feasibility study.

The analysis is supported by the finalised Development Application package, comprising the Town Planning Report and Appendices A–I, DA Form 1 and Land Owners' Consent (listed at the end of this document).

## Deal Overview

The project consolidates three adjoining freehold sites totalling 23,333 m² under a single **Community Titles Scheme (CTS)** to deliver 48 residential allotments within the Next Generation Neighbourhood Precinct. All three parcels are under contract, with settlements scheduled between May 2026 and March 2027.

The Development Application has been finalised for lodgement (Code Assessable Reconfiguring a Lot, 3 into 48 lots on a CTS, including a private road and access easement), informed by the Prelodgement Meeting held with Council on 28 January 2026 (DA/2026/0091).

Total development cost, inclusive of acquisition and all soft and hard costs, is forecast at **~$17.9m**. Gross realised value under forecast pricing is **~$24.4m**, producing a projected profit of **$5.5m** (net of GST) and a return on investment of **31.81%**. Under a conservative pricing scenario, the project retains an ROI in excess of 25%.

Key features of the opportunity:

- A concept plan aligned with Council's structure-plan intent, validated through the Prelodgement process and a subsequent structure-planning / traffic exercise.
- **Access resolved via Dorset Drive.** Council does not support direct access onto Torrens Road or River Drive (now signalised). A new private road connects to Dorset Drive — the option independently preferred by the traffic engineers.
- A lawful point of discharge for stormwater, via the existing Council drainage easement supplemented by **four engineered on-site detention (OSD) tanks** draining to the creek.
- A trunk sewer connection located on site within 3-7 Dorset Drive.
- In-house civil construction delivery by Pembroke Construction.
- Established town-planning representation (Steffan Harries) with extensive Moreton Bay City Council experience.

## Sales Guidance

Forecast sale prices were prepared by **B.E Property**, the project's appointed residential land sales agency, calibrated to comparable transactions in the Caboolture corridor as at March 2026 and reflecting individual lot size, frontage, aspect and position within the estate. A conservative column applies a uniform 5% reduction to stress-test against market softening.

> **Important disclosure — sales structure.** Under the Draft Loan Agreement, B.E Property is the financier's nominated sales partner and, together with the **builder-lender**, holds the **first and exclusive right to sell all 48 lots as House & Land Packages**. Each sale comprises (a) a land contract between the registered owner and the buyer at a B.E Property-determined market price (subject to the borrower's price approval, with a recommended pricing floor at the conservative column), and (b) a separate building contract between the lender and the buyer, with the **builder margin on the dwelling accruing to the lender, not to this project.** Pricing should therefore be understood as prepared by the project's appointed (related-party) sales channel rather than by an arm's-length independent valuer. The borrower retains a step-back right to convert the sales rights to non-exclusive if sales do not progress satisfactorily at plan sealing.

## Concept Plan

The concept plan delivers 48 residential allotments as a Community Titles Scheme, accessed via a **new private road connecting to Dorset Drive** (Modus "Option 3" — the preferred traffic outcome). Engineering documents describe a **10.5m wide road connection with a 6.5m carriageway** capable of two-way movement and 12.5m Heavy Rigid Vehicle (waste) servicing, with footpath and landscaping within the common property. *(Confirm the final internal road cross-section against prelodgement item 37, which sought a minimum 10m internal road.)*

The layout wholly avoids the central portion of the site affected by flooding and overland flow, concentrating developable land outside the constrained area. The existing Council drainage easement (constructed as part of the Torrens Road intersection upgrade) provides a lawful point of discharge, supplemented by four engineered OSD tanks. The plan is consistent with the structure-planning intent discussed at the Prelodgement Meeting (28 January 2026) and refined through subsequent traffic engineering.

**Car parking.** Council requires visitor parking at 0.5 spaces/lot (24 spaces); the plan allocates 26 spaces, exceeding the requirement.

**Density.** Land constrained by the drainage easement and the high/medium flood-hazard overlay is excluded from the developable-area calculation. The consolidated developable area across all three parcels is **20,149 m² (2.0149 ha)**, producing a net density of **23.8 lots/ha** — within the precinct's 10–25 lot/ha range. (The earlier 30-lot scheme over Torrens/River alone produced 27.77 lots/ha and was **not** supported at prelodgement; the three-parcel consolidation and redesign resolved both density and access.)

## Site Overlays

**Flood hazard.** The northern central portion of the Torrens Road parcel is affected by the high and medium flood-hazard overlay, within which Council does not permit residential development; the layout is configured accordingly. The applicable Defined Flood Event level is 10.5m AHD and the Flood Planning Level 11.25m AHD (750mm freeboard); allotment fill is designed to at least the adopted FPL. A **site-specific Flood Assessment (Bravo Consult, Appendix F)** confirms, on 1% AEP modelling, no material worsening of flood behaviour on adjoining or downstream land (level changes within 0.02m, velocity within 0.3m/s), with flood planning levels nominated for habitable floors.

> **Pending flood-model risk.** Council has advised (prelodgement items 28–30) that its **updated flood modelling is not yet reflected in the adopted Flood Planning Levels** and **may produce higher present-day flood levels**, and that the **northern portion has been re-defined from overland flow to medium/high flood hazard.** Council "strongly suggests" building to a higher level to avoid being "deemed flood affected at a future date." This is carried into the *Risk Analysis*.

**Overland flow.** An overland flow path traverses the central site. The northern flow falls within the area already excluded under the flood overlay; the southern flow is substantially contained within the existing drainage easement, minimising further impact on the developable footprint.

**Other overlays** identified at prelodgement include Acid Sulfate Soils (ASS report/management plan may be required for works below 5m AHD), Active Transport, Building Heights (max 12m), Road Hierarchy, Stormwater Catchments and Transport Noise Corridors (an acoustic assessment may be required given River Drive).

## Risk Analysis

**Rating methodology:** Score = Likelihood (1–5) × Consequence (1–5). Low (1–6), Medium (7–12), High (13–25).

| Risk | Category | L | C | Score | Rating |
|---|---|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|
| Inability to achieve forecast sale prices | Market | 3 | 3 | 9 | Medium |
| Performance-based planning departures (PO4 narrow lots; PO12/Figure 6 road link omitted) | Regulatory | 3 | 3 | 9 | Medium |
| Pending updated flood modelling raises FPL / re-maps hazard | Regulatory/Technical | 3 | 3 | 9 | Medium |
| Extended holding period and/or interest-rate movement | Financial | 4 | 3 | 12 | Medium |
| Civil construction cost escalation | Construction | 4 | 2 | 8 | Medium |
| Reliance on related-party / builder-lender sales channel | Commercial | 3 | 2 | 6 | Low |
| Unforeseen development costs | Financial | 3 | 2 | 6 | Low |
| Dorset Drive settlement (31 Mar 2027) vs. construction start | Delivery | 2 | 3 | 6 | Low |
| Third-party litigation or objections | Legal | 2 | 2 | 4 | Low |
| Protracted Council assessment timeframe | Regulatory | 2 | 2 | 4 | Low |

**Key mitigations.**

- *Sale prices.* Pricing prepared by the appointed sales agent (B.E Property) and stress-tested at −5% and −10%; product sits in the resilient affordable owner-occupier/investor segment.
- *Planning departures.* The application is Code Assessable (no public notification, lower objection risk). The Town Planning Report provides detailed performance-based justification for PO4 and PO12, relying on s1.7.4 of the scheme (examples are not exhaustive) and the supporting traffic advice (Option 3 preferred over the Figure 6 link). This is, however, a performance argument rather than acceptable-outcome compliance, and could attract an information request or condition; the 12-month DA allowance provides buffer.
- *Flood modelling.* The Bravo Consult flood report meets current adopted requirements; the residual risk is that newer (not-yet-adopted) modelling raises levels. Consider designing fill/FPL with margin to the latest modelling to protect future saleability.
- *Holding period / interest.* The schedule allows 12 months for DA; the 20%/15% facility makes early DA financially material. A six-month extension reduces ROI to 27.29% on the feasibility.
- *Construction cost.* In-house civil delivery gives direct cost control; +10% construction is absorbed at 28.95% ROI; severe combined downside retains 16.43% ROI.
- *Sales channel.* Borrower retains price-approval and a step-back right to convert exclusivity to non-exclusive at plan sealing if sales underperform.
- *Dorset settlement timing.* Confirm early-access/works arrangements so the access road is not gated by the 31 March 2027 settlement.

**Overall profile: Low to Medium.** Five risks are Medium (market pricing, planning departures, flood modelling, holding/interest, construction escalation); the remainder Low. No risks rated High. The feasibility sustains a positive return under a severe combined downside.
